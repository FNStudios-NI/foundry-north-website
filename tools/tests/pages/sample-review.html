Fork 1. Where the tide times come from

A key point nobody saw coming · after you left

The free tide feed went down twice during the run. I chose to save a copy of the tide tables each night and have the page read the copy, not the feed. checked the run record, both outages logged

Area
The data: where the page gets its tide times.
Problem it solves
When the feed is down, a page that reads it directly shows nothing at all.
Links in the chain
the free feed (outside us) → the nightly copy → the page → your phone
Cause and effect
If the feed is down at night, the copy keeps yesterday's times and the page says so; the page never goes blank.
1. Keep the nightly copy, or turn it another way

My recommendation: a. The feed's outages are the cause; the copy is what stops them reaching your phone.

If a: nothing changes · If b: a card in the next run takes the copy out · If c: I write the sign-up as your act, and nothing changes until you do it.

Fork 3. Which clock the times are in

I show every time in the harbour's own local time, with "harbour time" written beside it, not in your phone's time. guessed that you mostly look while you are at the harbour, from the plan's notes

Area
What the times on the page mean.
Problem it solves
A tide time is only useful in the clock of the place it happens; mixed clocks give a wrong time that looks right.
Links in the chain
the harbour's clock → the times on the page → you, at the harbour or planning from away
Cause and effect
If you are at the harbour, the times match your watch; if you plan from another time zone, you add the difference yourself.
3. Keep harbour time, or turn it another way

My recommendation: a. A tide happens in the harbour's clock; the label says so on every line.

If a: nothing changes · If b: a card in the next run switches every time to your phone's clock.
